In German, prepositions often merge with definite articles to make speech smoother. This is called a contraction.

Rule:

in + dem = im

Breakdown

in = in
dem = the (dative form of der/das)

So literally:

in dem Haus = in the house

When spoken/used normally, it contracts to:

im Haus = in the house

Why does this happen?

German avoids awkward sound combinations like:

in dem → in-dem (slower, heavier in speech)

So it contracts into:

im (short, natural, fast)

When do you use “dem”?

You only see dem when it is NOT contracted:

in dem Haus (formal / emphasis)
mit dem Mann
bei dem Arzt

But in everyday speech, Germans usually prefer contractions:

im Haus
beim Arzt (bei + dem)
vom Mann (von + dem)

Similar contractions
in + dem → im
an + dem → am
bei + dem → beim
von + dem → vom
zu + dem → zum

Simple memory rule

If you see:

in + dem → im

Just think:

“in + the (dative) becomes a single smooth word in everyday German.”
