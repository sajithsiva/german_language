Here are a few examples using **„dem Mann“** (“the man” in the dative case):

### 1. Giving something **to** the man

Ich gebe **dem Mann** ein Buch.
→ I give the man a book.

* **der Mann** = nominative (subject)
* **dem Mann** = dative (indirect object)

---

### 2. Speaking **with** the man

Ich spreche mit **dem Mann**.
→ I am speaking with the man.

The preposition **mit** always takes the dative case.

---

### 3. Going **to / at** the man (using *an dem → am*)

Ich stehe an **dem Mann** vorbei.
→ I stand next to the man / by the man.
(less common phrasing, but grammatically shows *an dem*)

More natural:
Das Kind steht neben **dem Mann**.
→ The child is standing next to the man.

---

### 4. Helping the man

Die Frau hilft **dem Mann**.
→ The woman helps the man.

The verb **helfen** requires the dative.

---

Notice how the article changes:

* **der Mann** → the man (subject)
* **den Mann** → the man (accusative)
* **dem Mann** → to/for the man (dative)

For masculine nouns like **Mann**, the dative article is **dem**.
