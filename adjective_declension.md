In German, adjectives change their endings depending on:

1. The **gender** of the noun (masculine, feminine, neuter)
    
2. The **case** (nominative, accusative, etc.)
    
3. The **article** used (ein, der, etc.)
    
This is called **adjective declension**.

### Base adjective

The dictionary form is:

**freundlich** = friendly

When it comes before a noun, it usually gets an ending.

### Nominative case (the subject)

| Gender    | Noun | Example                   |
| --------- | ---- | ------------------------- |
| Masculine | Mann | **ein freundlicher Mann** |
| Feminine  | Frau | **eine freundliche Frau** |
| Neuter    | Kind | **ein freundliches Kind** |

So:

- **Mann** is masculine → **freundlicher**
    
- **Frau** is feminine → **freundliche**
    
- **Kind** is neuter → **freundliches**
    

### Why the endings?

The article **ein/eine** does not always clearly show the gender:

|Gender|Article|
|---|---|
|Masculine|ein|
|Neuter|ein|
|Feminine|eine|

Notice that **ein** is used for both masculine and neuter. Therefore the adjective helps indicate the gender:

- ein freundlich**er** Mann
    
- ein freundlich**es** Kind
    

The endings **-er** and **-es** provide information that the article alone does not.

### More examples

- **ein junger Mann** = a young man
    
- **eine junge Frau** = a young woman
    
- **ein junges Kind** = a young child
    
- **ein großer Hund** = a big dog
    
- **eine große Katze** = a big cat
    
- **ein großes Haus** = a big house
    

### Simple rule for beginners

After **ein/eine** in the nominative case:

|Gender|Ending|
|---|---|
|Masculine|**-er**|
|Feminine|**-e**|
|Neuter|**-es**|

Examples:

- ein freundlich**er** Mann
    
- eine freundlich**e** Frau
    
- ein freundlich**es** Kind
    

As you progress, you'll learn other cases (accusative, dative, genitive), where the endings change again:

- Ich sehe **einen freundlichen Mann**. (accusative)
    
- Ich spreche mit **einem freundlichen Mann**. (dative)
