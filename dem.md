“dem” is one of the three distinct dative-case forms used in the German definite-article system corresponding to the English definite article “the”. It is specifically used with **masculine and neuter singular nouns** in the **dative** case.

Or simply, “dem is a dative form of the German definite article, used for both masculine and neuter singular nouns.”

Example:
- **mit dem Mann** → with the man
- **mit dem Kind** → with the child

- **Mann** is a **masculine singular noun**:
    - **der Mann** → the man
- **Kind** is a **neuter singular noun**:
    - **das Kind** → the child

In the **dative case**, both masculine and neuter definite articles change to **dem**.
The preposition **mit** (“with”) always requires the dative case.

| Nominative | Dative   |
| ---------- | -------- |
| der Mann   | dem Mann |
| das Kind   | dem Kind |

The three distinct dative forms in the German definite article system are:

- **dem** for masculine and neuter singular nouns
- **der** for feminine singular nouns
- **den** for plural nouns.

German does not use just one word for English word “the.”
Instead, German changes the word depending on gender, case, and number.

So all of these German words can mean English “the”:

| German | Possible English meaning |
| ------ | ------------------------ |
| der    | the                      |
| die    | the                      |
| das    | the                      |
| den    | the                      |
| dem    | the                      |
| des    | the                      |

These are different forms of the German definite article corresponding to English “the.”
